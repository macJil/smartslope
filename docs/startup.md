# Startup concept and business model

This is a proposed Startup framing for the prototype, not an operating business. The current repository has no payment flow, paying customers, signed pilot, user-count evidence, grant award, or revenue. The Philippine Startup Challenge guide asks teams to explain the problem, SDG priority, market, business model, solution, features, proposed investor ask, progress and founders.

## Problem and users

People who observe ground cracks, fallen soil/rock or drainage problems need a simple way to submit a location-linked report and see weather context for a small study area. Barangay staff or local disaster-risk personnel are the proposed institutional users who could review reports and organize follow-up. This is a project rationale, not a quantified local market study or claim that existing official systems fail.

## Solution and value

SmartSlope combines an offline map of Barangay Irisan, manually requested weather-provider snapshots, a transparent rainfall screening category, and an administrator-reviewed community report queue. Its current value is a demonstrable workflow for information sharing and academic learning. It does not replace official warnings or geotechnical assessment.

## SDG priorities

- **SDG 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities.** The project explores a local community reporting and preparedness-support workflow.
- **SDG 13: Climate Action.** The project uses weather context to discuss preparedness and risk communication.

The prototype does not measure SDG impact. These are proposed alignment areas.

## Target market and customer

Initial user group: residents and staff serving Barangay Irisan. Proposed paying customer: an LGU, barangay, or local disaster-risk office that chooses to commission setup and support. The team has not validated purchasing authority, willingness to pay or market size.

## Proposed business model

Potential model for later validation: a one-time setup and customization fee for a local deployment, followed by an annual maintenance/support agreement. A future operator could also charge for training, verified local data onboarding, and system updates. Sensor hardware, subscription pricing and commercial API use are outside the present prototype and would require separate cost and technical validation.

Potential funding/support ask: access to a barangay or DRRMO adviser for requirements feedback, permission to evaluate responsibly sourced local data, and mentorship or pilot resources for testing. No specific amount is claimed because hosting, support and hardware costs have not been estimated.

## Progress and evidence

The team has a working academic web prototype in branch `f1`, including login, a map, weather refresh and history, rainfall explanation, report submission, admin review and CSV workflows. The team reports that its local feature tests passed. This is software progress only. There are no reported partner commitments, deployed field pilot, paying customer, confirmed hazard dataset, validated risk accuracy or measured user outcomes.
